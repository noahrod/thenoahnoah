When I was a kid growing up in Mexico City, my education from elementary through high school was at a Catholic school called Instituto Don Bosco. It was run by the Salesians, and I always really enjoyed their style. They were relaxed, approachable, and focused on the motto "**hodie labor, cras fructus**"—which means "work today, enjoy the fruits later." They believed in teaching with kindness and heart, rather than using fear or strict discipline.

When I moved to Wisconsin, I naturally looked for a Salesian church near my home. Unfortunately, the closest one was across the border in Illinois, which was just too far of a drive. I ended up joining the Basilica of Saint Josaphat in Milwaukee instead. To be honest, it was a great setup for a while. The building is absolutely awesome to look at, but the real selling point was that it was right next door to Carnitas Don Lucho. This let me keep a cherished family tradition alive from my childhood: going to church on Sunday and then heading straight out for some delicious barbacoa or carnitas for breakfast.

But as I wrote in my last post, things took a frustrating turn when I needed my godparent certificate signed. The Basilica said **no**. I was incredibly sad and disappointed. I started to feel like the welcoming Church I remembered from my childhood was gone.

Thankfully, my wife and my sister-in-law stepped in and suggested that we just look for another church. That is when we found **St. Elizabeth Ann Seton in New Berlin, WI.**

Finding this parish felt like everything was coming full circle. I started reading about St. Elizabeth Ann Seton and quickly realized how much she had in common with St. John Bosco. Even though they lived in different times and places, their teachings are almost identical. Both saints believed that you have to win a person's heart before you can teach them anything. They didn't lead with snobby rules or harsh judgments; they led with love, social support, and a desire to help the community.

Going through this whole experience made one thing very clear to me: not all Catholics are the same. While some parishes might feel rigid or overly strict, there are groups and communities out there that are incredibly friendly, approachable, and forward-thinking. You just have to look for them.

And that brings me to the best news of all: **I finally got my god parent certificate signed!** The parish at St. Elizabeth Ann Seton signed it for me, and I am officially cleared for the baptism of my friend’s daughter.

I am so excited to take on this role. It means the world to me, and knowing that I have the backing of a community that shares my values makes it even better. I am ready to step up, be a great godparent, and make sure I am a positive, supportive presence in her life as she grows up.
